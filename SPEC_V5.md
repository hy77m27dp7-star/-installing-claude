## 1. Her clock, with Justin's rule

What she does: time moves for her the way it moves for a person, except inside a scene with him. When they are texting (the scene is apart or there is none), her day runs on: the hours pass, her mood fades on its clock, a night goes by and something small happened in it, and when he comes back after a real gap she knows how long it has been without making it his fault. When they are together in a scene, the moment holds: however long he is away, he comes back to the same moment in the same place, and she never mentions a gap.

Justin's rule (binding, his words: "time can pass on texting mode but not together mode... i might quit in the middle of a scene"). Story time runs only while the scene is APART. In a TOGETHER scene story time is FROZEN:
1. However long he is away, he returns to the same moment and place. The prompt's clock reads the instant the scene began (or the scene record's own `time` words when this scene wrote them; words carried over from the scene before are never read), and she never mentions a gap: no TIME SINCE section is ever built in together mode.
2. No events happen to her: the nightly her-day step and the arc step skip while a scene is held, and a dated step of hers or a life event that fell due inside the held span moves to the same distance after the span ends, so it still lies ahead of her (section 2 for beats, with a note on the want; `deferredInstant` below for life events). Nothing due after the span ends moves: her calendar is Portland's, so a Thursday she named stays Thursday.
3. Her mood does not fade and her friction does not heal while the scene is held: both measure story time, which excludes the held span.
4. No first texts fire while a scene is together (held or not: she is in the room with him), and no delayed-reply push fires while a scene is held. After a scene ends, the age of the last message a first text waits on is story time, so a scene paused for days never reads as days of silence.
5. The grounding (time of day, weather, outfit, today's rows) is the scene's held values, not the real now.
6. Ending a Together scene (the Apart switch, or the scene record moving to apart or none by any path: a promoted proposal, a restore, an import) restarts her clock FROM THAT MOMENT: the held span ends at the instant the record left together, and the span never counts toward any measure of absence. (A restore or an import of a snapshot that carries no clock rewinds the record itself, so its spans are re-derived from the restored versions, section "Export".)
7. In together mode the reply is never delayed (v2's real mode delays a reply by her day; she is in the room with him).

Shipped shape: the clock is derived from the scene record itself. Every run of consecutive scene versions whose status is `together` is one held span, opened at the `created_at` of its first version and closed at the `created_at` of the first later version whose status is not `together`. The spans are written to one small table lazily: every reader of the clock calls `syncStoryClock` first, which compares the table with `state_versions` and opens or closes a span when the record moved, so every path that moves the scene is caught without a hook in any of them. `storyNow` is strictly held inside a span (the scene record's `time` words carry narrative time within a scene); outside one it is the real now, so her calendar is Portland's and the weather is real. Absence is measured in story time: real elapsed time minus its overlap with every held span.

```ts
export function storyNow(clock: StoryClock): Date {
  if (clock.enabled && clock.open) return new Date(Date.parse(clock.open.frozen_at));
  return new Date(Date.parse(clock.real));
```

- `export function frozenOverlapMs(spans: readonly ClockSpan[], fromMs: number, toMs: number, realMs: number): number`: the sum over every span of `max(0, min(toMs, end) - max(fromMs, start))` with `start = Date.parse(frozen_at)` and `end = resumed_at ? Date.parse(resumed_at) : realMs`; a span with an unreadable start or end is skipped. Spans never overlap each other (the record is one sequence of versions), so the sum is the held time inside the interval.
- `export function storyElapsedMs(clock: StoryClock, fromIso: string | null | undefined, toIso?: string | null): number`, exactly:

```ts
export function storyElapsedMs(clock: StoryClock, fromIso: string | null | undefined, toIso?: string | null): number {
  const from = typeof fromIso === "string" ? Date.parse(fromIso) : NaN;
  const real = Date.parse(clock.real);
  const to = typeof toIso === "string" && toIso ? Date.parse(toIso) : real;
  if (!Number.isFinite(from) || !Number.isFinite(to) || to <= from) return 0;
  if (!clock.enabled) return to - from;
  return Math.max(0, to - from - frozenOverlapMs(clock.spans, from, to, real));
}
```

## 2. Arcs that go somewhere (dated beats on her wants)

What she does: a want of hers has steps with dates in story time: the open mic she is scared of has a sign-up by Tuesday and the night itself on Thursday. The steps coming up ride in WHAT YOU WANT under the want ("Coming up: the open mic, Thursday at 8pm"). When a step's date passes, either she says in the conversation how it went (and that is what happened, filed by the extractor), or the nightly pass decides it from who she is, how she has been and what happened with him about it (never a coin), and files it for the owner's keep. The outcome moves the want (a step forward or a setback in `want_log`), she brings it up herself (the callback picker offers it), and what he did about it (encouraged her, asked how it went, came, knew and forgot) stays with her for `arcMemoryDays`. The owner can write beats himself on the State page, a writer's room of one, with variants she chooses between. The tables keep the authored beat apart from its outcome for one reader, so the same beat could later run for many readers; v5 has one reader, `owner`.

Shipped shape: `arc_beats` (the authored step: title, kind, date, variants) and `beat_runs` (its run for one reader: that reader's due instant, which moves when a scene of his was held across it, and the outcome). A beat is created by the owner, by a promoted `want_beat` proposal (she named a dated step in the conversation), or never by a model on its own. An outcome arrives by a promoted `beat_outcome` proposal (the extractor, from her words; or the nightly arc step) or by the owner's Resolve.

Schema (0009_v5.sql):


## 3. Her view of him

What she does: she has a read on him, built from what he has actually done with her: you dodge when she asks about your family; you are loud in the mornings; you keep saying later; you asked before you kissed her. ## 4. State that moves (closes CONFLICTS 33 and 33a)

What she does: where they stand moves because of what happens between them, a step at a time, and she feels it: a relationship proposal that implies a change to status, trust, affection, attraction, friction, mood or nicknames changes those fields on promotion, never by a leap; a sore spot heals over days of story time and stops colouring things; a cooling off runs out on story time too; a nickname that stuck is added to the ones before it; and the scene record always says where they are.

The ladder (src/standing.ts). Rungs, in order: 0 `strangers`, 1 `talking`, 2 `friends`, 3 `seeing each other`, 4 `together`. Lateral states: `cooling off`, `on a break`, `over`. A status text maps to a rung or a lateral state by the first rule that matches its trimmed, lowercased form, in this order:

| Result | Matches |
|---|---|
| over | the text is exactly `over`, or matches `/\b(?:broke up\|broken up\|split up\|ended it\|it'?s over)\b/` |
| on a break | `/\bon a break\b/` |
| cooling off | `/\bcooling off\b/` |
| 4 together | `/\b(?:together\|official\|girlfriend\|boyfriend\|in a relationship\|partners?)\b/` |
| 3 seeing each other | `/\b(?:seeing each other\|dating\|going out\|seeing him\|seeing her)\b/` |
| 2 friends | `/\bfriends?\b/` |
| 0 strangers | `/\b(?:strangers?\|not met\|never met\|haven'?t met)\b/` |
| 1 talking | `/\b(?:talking\|texting\|getting to know\|acquaint\w*\|met)\b/` |
| unknown | anything else |

The live record's hand-written "seeing each other, early: met two days ago, one real date since" reads as rung 3 (the "seeing each other" rule comes before "met"). ## 5. Reply rhythm

What she does: her replies come in the shapes real texting comes in: one word; one short line; two or three short bubbles; now and then a longer one when she has something to say; in a Together scene, sometimes one action between asterisks, sometimes none, sometimes only an action. The next shape steers away from the last three, a run of long replies pulls the next one short, and the bookend habit (an action line, a few short lines, an action line) is steered out. All of it is an instruction in THIS MESSAGE; nothing ever cuts or rewrites what she wrote.

Shipped shape: the v3 shape cue (a cue on about 45% of turns, none on the rest) becomes a rhythm cue on every turn while `textureCuesEnabled` is on: a size from a texting distribution, an action choice in Together mode, and at most one extra (lowercase, the typo fix at `typoCueShare`, a voice note when allowed), each rolled from the turn's seed and steered by the shapes of her last three replies. `shape_uniform` reads the shape of what she said with action-only lines set aside. `length_pattern` still flags, and also feeds the cue. The retry keeps the same cue (same seed, byte-identical system text).
